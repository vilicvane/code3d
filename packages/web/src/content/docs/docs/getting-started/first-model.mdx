## Read the model

`box(36, 4, 24)` creates a box with dimensions along X, Y, and Z.
`.fillet(1)` rounds all its edges with radius 1. The result is bound to
`base`.

`cylinder(4, 14)` creates a cylinder of radius 4 and height 14 along Y.
Its relation places its bottom face on the base's top face. The
`offset(-10, 0, 0)` moves it within that relation's target frame.

`group([base, post])` brings the parts together and resolves their relative
placement. Grouping keeps separate parts; it does not fuse them into one solid.

## Follow the source

Place the cursor on `base` in the final `group` call to inspect the base.
Move to `post` to inspect the cylinder. Other parts may stay dimmed to provide
context. Move into the
`group` call to see the assembly.

A part viewed alone appears in its own local frame. You see its relation to
other parts when you inspect the composition that contains them.

## Change a dimension

Change the box's first dimension from `36` to `44`. The model recompiles as
you type. The source is the durable model, so the new dimension is already saved
to your workspace.

Place the cursor in the `box` arguments to explore the parameter tool.
Parameters that can be traced to editable source can also be changed there.

## Adjust the relation

Place the cursor in `offset(-10, 0, 0)`. Use the position tool to adjust
the relation visually. Watch the numeric source change with your adjustment.
The App writes the edit back to TypeScript and formats it.

Use Undo to revert an edit. During a position drag, Escape cancels the temporary
preview.

## Keep your own copy

The file you opened lives under `/examples`. That directory is managed by
Code3D and can be reset or refreshed with a new version of the application.
Create a file outside `/examples` and paste the source there to keep a model
you want to develop.
